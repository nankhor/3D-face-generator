# How to take the photos

The photos decide the result more than any setting. Ten minutes of care here saves hours later.

## Setup

- **Light:** soft and even. Stand facing a window on a cloudy day, or in the shade outside. No flash, no
  single lamp from the side, no harsh sun.
- **Background:** plain and not moving (a wall is ideal).
- **Camera:** a phone's main camera (not the selfie camera, not the ultra-wide). Ask someone else to take
  them. Keep the same zoom for every photo.
- **Distance:** about 1 to 1.5 metres, head and shoulders in the frame, head roughly in the middle.

## The person

- Neutral face, mouth closed, eyes open, looking straight ahead (not at the camera) the whole time.
- Hair off the forehead and ears if possible (tie it back or use a headband). Ears matter for the shape.
- Glasses off.
- Sit on a chair and keep still; the photographer moves, not the person.

## The photos (30 to 50)

1. **A ring at eye level:** walk a half circle from one ear to the other, a photo every 10 to 15 degrees
   (about 15 photos). Include both exact profiles.
2. **A ring from slightly below:** same half circle with the phone at chin height pointing up a little
   (about 10 photos). This shows the underside of the jaw and the neck.
3. **A ring from slightly above:** same with the phone above head height pointing down (about 8 photos).
4. **Beard extras:** one straight-on and one exact side profile **with the chin lifted**, and one photo
   from under the jaw looking up. These show where the jaw is under the beard.
5. **Close-ups:** two or three sharp close-ups of the face from the front for skin detail.

Every photo must be sharp. Delete blurry ones.

## After the photos

Put them in one folder (JPG or PNG; export HEIC to JPG). Then, in the line editor, draw the jawbone on one
side photo and one front photo: press on your jaw to feel where the bone is, and draw the bone, not the edge
of the beard.
